(`/workspace/notes/repo_notes/`). Tags: **[FATAL]** = the design as written would produce wrong numbers;
**[MAJOR]** = the interpretation would not follow; **[MINOR]** = a fix or a caution.

## Part I

### [FATAL] 1. The finite-difference precision plan fails under the RHUT "fp32" regime
RHUT's `Fp32Residual` upcasts only the residual stream. Every block still runs under bf16 autocast, so matmuls,
attention and the GDN kernels see bf16 operands (`common/scripts/hooks_fp32.py` docstring). bf16 has ~2⁻⁸ ≈ 0.4%
relative resolution. A centred difference at ‖εΔ‖ ≈ 10⁻³‖x‖ sits **below** that resolution, so T̄_H and T̄_full would be
rounding noise. ekko measured exactly this: bf16 central differences have cos 0.26 to the true JVP at ε=0.001 and 0.93
at ε=0.01 (FINDINGS F5).

**Fix.** Run the forward-from-ℓ in true fp32. Cache the block-ℓ outputs in bf16, then convert blocks ℓ₀..62 (and
block 63, the final norm and lm_head for ψ) to fp32, with TF32 disabled. For ℓ₀ = 36 that is ≈45 GB, which fits the
H100. The Richardson check then becomes meaningful, and ε ≈ 10⁻³ is appropriate.

### [FATAL→MAJOR] 2. The Stage-0 gate cannot be run as specified on fresh hosts, and 0.999 is the wrong bar
- J_CB's checkpoint records `docs_consumed: 25` but not which docs. The gate therefore rests on a hypothesis: the
  first 25 docs of pile-10k, tokenized to 128 with or without BOS.
- On fresh hosts, agreement is limited by genuine per-prompt variation, not code. Same-token J rows on different
  prompts have cos 0.45 at L36 and 0.95 at L60 (phrase_J 002). A 32-host estimate vs a 25-doc estimate would land
  around cos 0.9–0.95 at L40. That can't validate the target layer, the fold or the aggregation.
- J_CB was fitted with a bf16 backward at an unknown `dim_batch`. The shape floor is relerr 0.004 at L56 and cos
  ≈0.998 at L36 (phrase_J 002).

**Fix.** Make the gate "cos ≥ 0.99 and relative norm within 3% at ℓ ≥ 40, on the hypothesised fit docs". Use the gate
to *discover* the convention: BOS or not, target range including or excluding the last position. The fresh-host number
is then reported as a noise estimate, not as a gate.

### [MAJOR] 3. Single-cell patches make P≈0 by redundancy, and the carrier prediction is pre-baked
RHUT's leverage numbers come from **span** interventions: all carrier positions, all blocks ≥36. A single (ℓ, t) patch
leaves every other carrier position, and every other layer at other positions, carrying the clean content.
- P_cell at carriers will be ≈ 0.07 of the ceiling divided by the number of carrier positions, i.e. near zero.
- In a host, the perturbed position is the *only* copy, so T̄ ≫ P follows from redundancy alone.
- As a result, "carrier G_ctx dominant, not routed" is guaranteed by construction. It can't distinguish routing
  from redundancy, and Stage 3's source+carrier joint ablation doesn't touch *within-role* redundancy.

**Fix.** The primary unit is the **role span** (all positions of a role, at layer ℓ):
- Δ_span = the stacked differences;
- P = the patch of the whole span at ℓ;
- A = the gradient contracted over the span;
- the host counterpart perturbs a contiguous span of the same length.

Every identity still holds, because the tangent is linear in the span. Keep per-cell numbers as a secondary
"redundancy profile", including leave-one-in (all cells of the role donor except one).

### [MAJOR] 4. The "exact identity explains readable-but-unused" claim is overstated
- The identities are telescoping sums (a−d = (a−b)+(b−d)). They are exact for any four numbers. The content is only in
  whether each term is measured on the same functional.
- **ψ (behaviour) cannot be host-averaged.** It is item-specific. So T̄/S̄/G_ctx/G_hcurv/G_hz exist only for φ (future
  logits of a token). The decomposition explains *why the lens's reading differs from the in-context effect on future
  token logits*. It does not explain why readable content fails to move the answer.
- The link to RHUT's question is the P^int × P^ψ table. That table is a crosstab, not an identity. Keep it (it's good),
  but frame it accurately.

### [MAJOR] 5. Pile hosts vs chat-templated items: G_ctx includes distribution shift
The items are chat-rendered two-hop prompts; the hosts are pile text. Without the chat-host arm, G_ctx = context
routing + redundancy + distribution shift, and it can't be interpreted. **Make the chat-host arm mandatory**, e.g.
Qwen's own responses to generic prompts, rendered with the same template.

### [MINOR] 6. S̄(α) at pile hosts adds a whole in-distribution Δ to an unrelated state (off-manifold)
The α-profile mixes model curvature with off-distribution breakage. The cleaner curvature measurement is **in-context**
(P(α) = f(h) − f(h − αΔ) for α ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}), which G_curv already sits next to. Keep host S̄ only at α ≤ 0.5, and
flag it.

### [MINOR] 7. Items
The released 12 two-hop items are "too easy / answer linearly related to the intermediate" (Nanda). RHUT already found
the answer plane carries more than the intermediate plane (0.37 vs 0.19). Report φ^ans alongside φ^int, which the spec
does. Scale to the 35 admitted items before reading anything off the φ^int result.

### What survives from Part I, ranked
1. **The matrix-free lens** (Stage 0, fixed per 1–2): infrastructure for everything else.
2. **G_hz (horizon)**: cheap, new, and generalises. "Readable at late positions" may partly be forecasting beyond the
   sequence end. It needs only T̄_full and T̄_H on the same hosts, not the lens matrix.
3. **G_curv in context**, i.e. the P vs A vs path-integral check. It decides whether AtP-type tools are valid at these
   sites, which is useful beyond this project.
4. **The span-level P^int × P^ψ table.**
5. G_ctx: only after items 3 and 5 of this list are fixed.

## Part II

- **II.1 write directions (prior 50%, the best-designed item).**
  - Keep it. The Haufe filter/pattern framing is correct, the polarization identity for SᵀF̄S is correct
    (KL(s_i+s_j) − KL(s_i) − KL(s_j) = s_iᵀF̄s_j to second order), and matched damage is the right metric.
  - The KL measurements at small doses need the same fp32 upper stack (#1), or they are rounding noise.
  - Two load-bearing premises come from results that aren't available to me: "G&W held states swap at 58–64%" (a
    source I don't know) and "Chen's ridge sweep 25→40%" (phrase-lens ships no results). Please point me to them.
  - Needs only a covariance estimate (Ledoit–Wolf), which is not model fitting.
- **II.2 Oracle swappability.**
  - `agu18dec/local-workspace` is **private** (401 today). The public `agu18dec/olens_and_ar` ships `ar_ptag_pooled/`
    (the reconstructor LoRA + heads) and `olens_s3d_rl600/`, so it's feasible.
  - Caveat: the reconstructor predicts the activation *preceding* a phrase. That is an emission state, so its swaps
    risk being answer steering (the RHUT trap). Include the answer-row control.
- **II.3 is fitting.** It's a J refit with per-prompt saving, ~2–4 GPU-hours for 16 layers on the H100 (ekko measured
  183 s per prompt). It's cheap and legitimate, but it is a fit.
- **II.4a context-agreement lens: I'd lower the prior to 15–20%.** Normalising readouts by a noise term already failed
  here: calibrated-z (E6) cut pass@10 everywhere. t-ranking systematically favours context-robust generic tokens. It
  also depends on II.3.
- **II.4b** is fine as conditional.
- **II.5 lag buckets: the best cheap kill test in the document.**
  - An efficiency fix: you don't need one host position per bucket. Perturb *all* valid positions with independent
    random signs sₚ ∈ {±1}, then estimate lag δ as Σ_{t'} s_{t'−δ}·Δy_{t'}. Cross terms cancel in expectation, so one
    forward pair per host gives every lag at once.
  - Also report the rank under J_CB and under the n=1000 J_NP. WSB's .07 is J_NP with a cosine readout.
- **II.5 J^Q**: fine, cheap, keep the pretest kill.
- **II.6 MTP.** HF doesn't load the MTP block, so it would have to be reimplemented. Feeding J̄h in place of the final
  hidden state is off-distribution for a block conditioned on emb(x_{t+1}). Low prior, and hours of engineering.
- **II.7 probes/DAS are fitting.** Probes are cheap. DAS needs gradient training through a 27B model, which is the
  expensive part.

### An internal contradiction in the evaluation rules
"Reproduce the bench's own J numbers (brew .86, basic MT .24)" and "matched n: J_CB vs R_CB, never the n=1000 arm"
conflict. The bench's J arm *is* the n=1000 Neuronpedia lens with a cosine readout under the any-cell rule.
**Order:**
1. reproduce with J_NP + cosine + any-cell, which validates the harness;
2. switch to J_CB / R_CB, per-layer, paired.
