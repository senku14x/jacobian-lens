## Part 0. The two things to keep separate throughout

1. **The measurement claim**: the J-lens reads out a privileged subframe of residual-stream directions, and that subframe carries the model's report-relevant and intermediate-reasoning content.
2. **The organizational claim**: that subframe is a *unified* workspace with selection, capacity limits, and broadcast, i.e. a single functional structure rather than a collection of independently useful directions.

The paper's evidence for (1) is strong and much of it is causal. The evidence for (2) is real but mostly structural/correlational, and several of its key measurements are partly guaranteed by how the J-lens is constructed. Both external technical commentaries (Eleos: "privileged set vs privileged stream"; Nanda: "I am persuaded by the scientific claim [a cognitive space exists], not by all the fine details of section 4") land on the same split.

# A. Detailed technical notes

## A.1 The Jacobian lens: what it computes

**Definition (§2.1).** For each layer ℓ:

J_ℓ = E_{t, t' ≥ t, prompt} [ ∂h_{final,t'} / ∂h_{ℓ,t} ]

Readout: lens(h_ℓ) = softmax(W_U · norm(J_ℓ h_ℓ)). The "J-lens vector" for token v is the row of W_U J_ℓ (strictly, W_U diag(γ) J_ℓ because the final RMSNorm's gain sits between them; the norm's division by rms(J h) is a per-activation scalar that does not change rankings).

**What the code actually does (fitting.py).** For each output dimension i, a one-hot cotangent e_i is injected at *every valid target position simultaneously* and backpropagated once. The gradient landing at source position p is therefore Σ_{p' ≥ p} ∂h_final[p', i]/∂h_ℓ[p], a **sum over future targets**, which is then **averaged over source positions** p (excluding the first 16 positions, treated as attention-sink outliers, and the final position), then averaged per element over prompts. Consequences:

- Early source positions get more weight in the row norm (they have more future targets). Rankings are norm-insensitive, but interventions that scale J-vectors by "mean residual norm" (data/experiments/README) inherit this.
- Fitting uses 128-token sequences. The cross-position ("future") component of J is estimated from futures of ≤ 112 tokens. Applying the lens to 8k-token transcripts (blackmail, character drift) extrapolates a short-range estimate.
- Paper default for Sonnet 4.5: gradient of the **penultimate** layer, not the final one ("including the last layer can increase noisy artifacts"; "the final block is heavily specialized for calibrating next-token predictions"). Code default is the final layer with a `target_layer` option.
- Gradients flow through QK by default. A frozen-QK variant "can increase the causal effect" (A.7). Future-only and self-only variants exist; self-only is close to logit-lens in spirit.
- Per-element mean aggregation; median and outlier filtering considered (within-prompt exclusion of positions with residual norm > Nσ above mean; cross-prompt exclusion of prompts with Jacobian Frobenius norm > Nσ above mean). ~10 prompts already beat logit/tuned lens; 1000 sequences × 128 tokens is the default. Restricting the data distribution (burn-in exclusion, masking non-alphanumeric next tokens) gave no improvement. Nanda used 25 prompts on Qwen 3.6 27B and 4 prompts on a 397B MoE.
- Jacobians stored in fp16.

**What it is measuring, precisely.** J_ℓ is the *mean tangent map* from layer ℓ to the pre-unembed residual, averaged over natural operating points. ⟨v_token, h⟩ is the average first-order change in that token's eventual logit (at the same or any later position) from adding h at layer ℓ. So a J-vector is "the direction that, on average over contexts, most efficiently increases the chance that the model eventually says this token."

Two things follow that the paper does not spell out:

- **J_ℓ h is not what the model computes from h.** The lens applies a *tangent* map to a *full* activation; that is a linear extrapolation from the operating point to the origin. The paper's own data show the consequence: J-lens is the *worst* of the three lenses at predicting the model's next-token distribution at every depth (A.6). The authors frame this as a feature (it does not "skip ahead"), which is fair, but it means J-lens readouts are dispositions, not predictions.
- **J-vectors are gradient directions, hence plausibly approximately whitened/discriminant directions.** The template-lens section (A.9.1) invokes Stein's lemma: E[∇g] = Σ⁻¹ E[g (x−μ)], so the J-vector for token w ≈ Σ⁻¹(μ_w − μ), an LDA direction, not a mean-difference direction. The paper uses this only to motivate the template lens approximating the J-lens, and states that both assumptions (Gaussian activations; g depending only on x) fail. Treat "J-vectors ≈ whitened directions" as a **heuristic, testable by E.4**, not as established. If it holds, it explains much of the paper's "privileged" results (see A.3, A.4) and is the most important methodological lens through which to read them.

**Assumptions required to read a J-vector as "a verbalizable representation of concept X":**

1. First-order sufficiency: the averaged linearization captures how X-content influences eventual verbalization.
2. Context-invariance of the direction: the per-context Jacobians share a common direction for token X (the paper notes heavy tails and considers medians/filters; agreement across variants is good but not perfect).
3. Token = concept: X has a single-token name whose promotion direction coincides with the model's working representation of X. The paper's own evidence that this fails: number words swap 0/48 (A.14); multi-token intermediates degrade sharply (A.9); the highest-κ SAE features have *higher* MLP gain than J-vectors in early workspace layers, which the authors read as "J-vectors only partially capture the true workspace directions" (§4.3 fn).
4. Uniqueness of coordinates: the J-vectors are overcomplete and correlated; "J-space coordinates" are pseudo-inverse (two-vector swaps) or greedy gradient-pursuit (occupancy, decomposition) solutions and are not unique. The attribution-graph appendix admits the pursuit "can identify a near-miss correlate of a concept that is still forming."

**Versus other methods (§2.4, A.5, A.6, related work):**

| Method | Map | Objective | Failure mode noted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logit lens | J = I | none | uninterpretable below ~L58; agrees with J-lens in late workspace; authors call it "quite useful in practice" |
| Tuned lens | affine regression h_ℓ → h_final | match output | "skips ahead" to the answer; early-layer advantage is entirely the bias term (ignores input); worst at recovering intermediates |
| J-lens | mean Jacobian | causal first-order effect on eventual output | worst at next-token prediction; single-token vocabulary; noisy directions |
| Probes | supervised mean-diff or logistic | separate labelled classes | correlational; one concept per probe; carries high-variance nuisance directions |
| SAEs | learned dictionary | sparsity + reconstruction | needs interpretation; ~85% of features are not J-aligned (A.18) |
| Steering vectors | mean-diff | contrastive | correlational; the paper shows their non-J component carries ~93% of variance but ~0 report effect |
| Template lens (A.9.1) | whitened mean-diff at "about to say w" positions | LDA | inherits tuned-lens skip-ahead; needs enumerated vocabulary |
| Hernandez relation Jacobians | per-relation Jacobian | subject→object | single relation; J-lens is to the output |
| NLAs / activation oracles | trained verbalizer (+ reconstructor) | free text | confabulation risk; expensive; J-lens is cheap-and-grounded end of spectrum |

Quantitatively (A.6): J-lens beats logit lens on all six intermediate-recovery distributions (multihop 50, multilingual 54, order-of-ops 55, poetry 52, typo 96, association 50 items), with the biggest margins on multilingual, order-of-ops, poetry, typo; tuned lens recovers almost nothing on association and poetry (uninformative next token at readout position). Ablating J-lens directions induces ~2× the output KL of logit/tuned directions on multihop; swaps flip outputs more often at all three model scales. Inter-lens agreement by layer: pre-workspace (0–33) no readout agreement, J-lens near-orthogonal to both (low-rank); early workspace (38–58) J-to-logit cosine climbs 0→0.7 while KL stays high; late workspace (62–92) logit converges to J, tuned stays outlier; motor layers all collapse. Nanda's replication on Qwen: multilingual and typo replicate; poetry and arithmetic failed (attributed to model capability or experimenter error); on multihop, *answer swaps dominated intermediate swaps*, which he attributes to easy, linearly-related pairs (France/Paris).

**Known failure modes and limitations (paper's own list + ours):** single-token vocabulary; "bag of concepts" with no binding; readouts at some cells are uninterpretable and this is uncharacterized; workspace/motor boundary is post-hoc; early layers may be a lens artifact; no mechanism for entry; interventions amplify the noise in the direction (Nanda); the fit corpus is pretraining-like, so post-training-specific representations may be under-served; mere mention of a concept loads it (A.10), which is a large false-positive channel for monitoring.

## A.2 What "J-space" is (§2.3, A.8)

Not a subspace. The J-vectors span the full residual stream (n_vocab > d_model). J-space is defined as the union over all k-subsets S of J-vectors of the nonnegative cone they span, for a chosen sparsity k (typically ≤ 25). The "J-space component" of an activation is its nearest point in that union of cones, found greedily by gradient pursuit. (The LaTeX formula in A.8 writes "span" where the text says nonnegative combinations; the operational code uses nonnegative pursuit.)

So J-space is **an operational construct with a free parameter k**, and "in the J-space" means "well approximated by few J-vectors." Statements like "the J-space accounts for <10% of variance" (§4.2) are statements about a k≈25 greedy fit relative to a random-direction control of the same size. The A.8 formalization (distance functions d_F(x) between unions of cones, Δ_μ(F,G) as RMS difference of distances over data, one-sided containment D_μ(F→G) = ||(d_G − d_F)_+||) is proposed but not used to compare any two candidate spaces in the paper. Under it, adding multi-token vectors yields a strictly larger space and limits are well-defined.

**Dimensionality/capacity characterizations:** (i) effective linear dimension of the J-vector set rises sharply at ~L38 to span most of the residual stream (§4.1d); (ii) occupancy (k at which marginal reconstruction gain drops to that of random directions) ≈ 25 median across the band, near 0 before it; (iii) excess variance explained ≤ 10%; (iv) ~15% of SAE features are J-aligned at peak after excluding motor features (A.18); (v) functional list capacity ≈ 6 unrelated items pooled over the band, 1–2 at a single layer (A.16).

**Stability:** across layers, CKA of J-vector Gram matrices shows three blocks (early/middle/late), "in some models more gradual, sometimes containing sub-blocks; sharpness exaggerated by subsampling"; Nanda sees "two or three overlapping bands" on Qwen. Across models: key results corroborated on Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5, Opus 4.6; base vs post-trained models both have a J-space; Qwen 3.6 27B replicates the core readout/swap phenomena more weakly. Across prompts: the readout at a position depends strongly on task framing (§3.2, §3.5), which is the point. Across tokens: top-1 autocorrelation across positions is elevated in the band (abstract content persists) and near-null outside it.

**What supports "workspace" over "collection of decodable features":** the swap-generalization result (one vector, many functions, §3.4), the layer-timed emergence of intermediates in the required order with matching patching layers (§3.3, A.24.1), the ignition-like commitment at the band onset (§4.1.1), the broadcast-head ablation (§4.3), the competition result (A.17). What does *not* distinguish them: verbal report, occupancy, variance explained, the MLP-gain result on its own (see A.10 below). Eleos's formulation is the right one: the paper establishes a privileged *set*; a privileged *stream* (shared entry/exit mechanisms, holistic state influence) is suggested, not established.

## A.3 Verbal report (§3.1)

- Think-of-a-category (14 categories): J-lens at the colon before the answer correlates with output ranking (Spearman over 10 candidates, 3 band layers); correlation rises toward the band's end. Swap (source = spontaneously chosen item, target = same-category item outside the top-10, at all positions) moves the target into the top of the output distribution (88% top-5 for pure J-vectors).
- Injected thought (n=100 concepts; median reciprocal rank vs steering strength): inject one J-vector across all user-turn tokens; the model reports it when asked to introspect (majority of trials), but does *not* say the word at other assistant positions. This position selectivity is the non-trivial part: it shows J content at user positions is read only under the report instruction. Origin: *elephant* was noticed in the readout at the comma after "If so" in the user prompt.
- Privilege test: a "Tell me about X" mean-diff concept vector (mean-subtracted over 100 concepts) is split into a k=16 J-component (6–7% of variance) and remainder, both rescaled to equal magnitude. Swapping along J-component: 59% top-5; remainder: 5%; pure J-vector: 88%. Injection: J-component ≈ pure J-vector; remainder few reports even at several× strength. Clamping J-coordinates to clean values kills the remainder's residual effect (0 in swap, ~0 in injection).

Reading: the causal effect of a mean-diff concept vector on report lives almost entirely in its small gradient-aligned component. Given A.1's whitening heuristic, this is the expected behaviour of a whitened vs unwhitened direction, and it is nearly guaranteed for an effect on *output* because the J-vector is defined by its output effect. The clamp control matters more: it shows the non-J route to report goes *through* J coordinates rather than around them.

## A.4 Directed modulation (§3.2, A.10, A.11, A.12)

**Interventions performed:** none are interventions in this section. All results are readouts under prompt manipulation. The only causal element is in A.12 (a probe measurement) and the selectivity swaps of §3.5.

**Setups:** (a) copy an unrelated sentence while "concentrating on citrus": "orange" top-1 across layers at "ook" (lemon also); earlier layers show "fruit, thoughts, imagine, thinking, focused, imagery." (b) evaluate 3²−2 while copying: "arithmetic/math" → "nine" → "seven" (+ answer, equals) across layers at the same copied token. (c) silently count characters: "lines, sentence, length" → "forty" (39, 41, 43, fifty, thirty nearby) at the newline. (d) Systematic: focus vs ignore vs none; baseline ≈ 0; focus substantial and rising with model size; ignore below focus but above zero ("white bear"). (e) Paired-question protocol: the same stimulus preceded by "predict the next word" vs "name the property" (part of speech, tense; appendix adds dialect, register, number, tone); the property label appears in J-space only under the naming question, though next-word predictions respect the property under both.

**The appendix materially deflates (d):** A.10 adds *mention* (name X with no instruction) and *don't-think*. On category and math tasks, **mention ≈ focus**; *don't-think* ≈ mention; only *ignore* (framing X as irrelevant) suppresses. So most of the "summoning" is priming by context, and the demonstrable top-down control is *downward* modulation under one phrasing. The main text says "a bare mention can prime it almost as strongly," but the headline framing is top-down control. Line-width task behaves differently on every condition (harder; Opus 4.5 precision 14–56% across phrasings).

**A.12 is the strongest result in this section:** "Imagine this JavaScript is Python" raises "python" in the J-lens by several SD at stimulus positions while a J-orthogonalized Python-code probe (mean-diff probe with top-25 J directions projected out) is unmoved (~1 SD); a real Python stimulus moves the probe 3–6 SD and sometimes does not move the J-lens at all (past-tense sentence: probe +6 SD, "past" far down the ranking). 4 categories × n=24. Interpretation: instructions write a *label* into the J-subframe without re-encoding the stimulus. This is a genuine dissociation and the best evidence that J-content and "underlying representation" are different objects. Closest prior result: Bogdan & Lindsey's entity "slots", where the same entity information is reportable only when in the current-entity slot (see D.1).

**Represent vs use.** Nothing in §3.2 shows the summoned content is *used*. The paired-question protocol shows the label enters when it will be needed for report. The dual-task appendix shows that summoned arithmetic content competes with summoned concept content at the token level (A.17), which is weak evidence that it occupies a shared resource. Whether "orange" loaded by an instruction is causally available to a later question ("what were you thinking about?") is not tested, and whether mention-loaded "orange" is the same direction as focus-loaded "orange" is not tested.

**Mechanism for persistence across the copied sentence: not addressed.** See Part B.

## A.5 Internal reasoning and the swap experiments (§3.3, A.6, A.24)

**Intervention.** Given source token s and target t: V = [v_s v_t], c = V⁺h, h' = h + V(σ(c) − c), optionally scaled by α. Applied at *every token position* over a *band of layers*, "clamped" (re-applied at each layer so the model cannot restore the original). Orthogonal complement of span{v_s, v_t} untouched, but correlated J-vectors (arachnid, web, legs, ...) are partially altered because they are not orthogonal to that span.

**Results.** spider→ant flips 8→6; fight→light changes the *earlier* word (coming→morning) in the couplet; Chinese antonym flips via English J-vectors (big/bigger→long/longer: 大→长; *Chinese* also present in lens); bandit repeat/switch flips in both directions (only the contextually appropriate strategy word is present though both are in the prompt); 50 two-hop prompts: 54/70/70% top-1 flips (Haiku/Sonnet/Opus 4.5). Answer-smuggling control: intermediate swaps take effect a median ~17% of depth earlier than answer swaps. Probe decomposition (n=90; probe = mean over prompts implying same intermediate via different cues minus grand mean; k=25 J-component ~10–15% variance): raw J swap 60%, probe J-component 61%, probe remainder 28% → 6% when J-coordinates clamped (clamped set = tokens in the probe's J-component plus the token naming the concept). Arithmetic (4+17)*2+7 on Opus 4.5: 21, 42, 49 absent in first third, climb together, separate ~L71 in computation order (21 first, 42 ~8 layers later, 49 final layers), and mean-difference patching (A.24.1), a non-J-lens method, is causally effective at exactly those layers (21 ~L71, 42 ~L79, 49 last few).

**What is causal here.** These are the paper's strongest causal results: intervention on a named intermediate, never in input or output, redirects the answer in the direction the swapped-in intermediate implies, and doing so at the intermediate's layer band works earlier than doing so at the answer's. Nanda: "I basically don't have good alternative hypotheses" for bandit and poetry; the arithmetic layer-band result with non-lens corroboration "rules out a fair amount of ways this could be spurious."

**Differential swap (A.24.2).** Swapping 42→63 raises P(70) (= 63+7); swapping 21→63 raises P(133) (= 63×2+7). The same value inserted into different intermediates yields different answers because different computations are applied downstream. This is the cleanest evidence in the paper that intermediates are *operated on* rather than pushed toward output.

**Remaining alternatives and caveats.**
- Linear-composition alternative (Paris ≈ France + "capital-of"): argued against by the layer-timing control, but Nanda found answer swaps dominate on Qwen for easy pairs. The result is real on Claude models; its generality is a question.
- "At every position" includes the prompt tokens where the entity is first represented; the intervention is not localized. Sufficiency, yes; "the J-space is *the* channel," no, because the non-J remainder does move the answer in 28% of cases before clamping, i.e. re-entry from non-J routes exists and the clamp is what shuts it.
- The attribution graph (A.24.2) qualifies the arithmetic showcase: the first addition (4+17) happens *outside* the J-space (swapping 4 or 17 J-coordinates does nothing; "21 appears already computed", receiving some input from a *seventeen* node and the remainder at "17"); the literal operand 2 never appears as a node; the ×2 is fused into "doubled" (swap doubled→tripled → 70); +7 is partly outside (swapping 7 raises the counterfactual but does not displace 49). "Most of the influence on the output flow[s] through remainder nodes." So even in the best case the J-space carries the verbalizable *skeleton* of the computation.
- Success rates are 54–70% on curated single-token two-hop prompts, ~40% (α=1) on the generalization battery, 0% on numbers.

## A.6 Flexible generalization (§3.4, A.13)

One swap (France→China) applied identically across four templates per category (countries, months, animals, number words); 76/192 top-1 at α=1, 101/192 at α=2. Countries 42/48; months partial; animals rare; numbers 0/48. Failures correlate with low "workspace loading" (cosine of residual with the source's J-vector, averaged over argument and readout positions). Among failures, top-1 typically stays the original answer with the target further down. Post-hoc explanation: either the model computes over small integers outside the workspace, or its integer representation is not token-aligned. Note that the workspace-loading explanation is fitted after the fact and that α=2 helping suggests the swap magnitude is under-calibrated (pseudo-inverse coordinates on a two-vector dictionary underestimate the concept's true loading when the concept is distributed over correlated J-vectors).

## A.7 Selectivity (§3.5, A.15)

- Language passages (n=8): swap Spanish→French *across the question tokens only*. Explicit report and flexible questions (famous author, word for "hello", pre-Euro currency) flip on essentially every trial (García Márquez→Hugo, Hola→Bonjour, Peseta→Franc); continuation and anomaly detection (spliced foreign sentence) do not. *Spanish* is present in the lens at comparable rates in all four conditions. **Confound:** the passage tokens still carry Spanish in both J and non-J form; continuation and intrusion detection can read the passage tokens directly by attention, which the intervention never touched. The clean version swaps over passage tokens too. Until then, "automatic processing does not route through J-space" is not separated from "automatic processing reads the unswapped positions."
- Line counting (n=11): numbers absent from J-space under linewrap, present under report (~20 positions), most present under first-letter-of-count-word. Swaps 40s→60s move the reported answer ("46"→"65"; "F"→"S") and not the wrap point. Here the readout absence is the evidence; the swap is vacuous where the coordinates are ~0. Combined with Gurnee et al. 2025 (count features exist outside), this is the cleaner of the two.
- Ablation battery: top-10 J-vectors zeroed at every position over light/medium/heavy layer ranges, **excluding tokens in the clean top-10 output** (this exclusion is specific to this battery; see A.11). Positive control: multihop near-ceiling → ~0 under heavy. Survive: MMLU-MC, odd-one-out, SQuAD extractive, sentiment, CoLA. Fail (below unablated Haiku 4.5): Caesar, analogy, summarization, TriviaQA, multi-hop, translation, sonnet. GSM8K with CoT far more robust than direct (interpreted as externalizing the workspace to the page). Pretraining top-1 agreement stays high; disrupted positions are ones needing an assembled abstract characterization of context (topic of a clinical paper, historical setting, botanist nationality, diagnostic vs physics framing).
  **Deflationary reading:** the surviving tasks are choice/extraction from context; the failing tasks require *generating* content from internal knowledge. Removing the top internal vocabulary-promotion directions at every position will preferentially break the latter regardless of any workspace organization. This does not contradict the paper's claim, it re-describes it; what it removes is the license to call the surviving tasks "automatic" in a mechanistically interesting sense.
- A.15 inclusion/exclusion (n=63 items where the model both names with p≥0.85 and avoids with p≤0.15): ablating the implied concept's J-vector at *early* band layers (L38–54) leaves naming intact but raises avoidance failure ~5×; *late* (L75–92) ablation lowers production under both. A same-category control vector at equal magnitude has a smaller effect. This is a nice functional layer dissociation (early J-content is used for inhibition, late for production) and it also shows "the J-space" is not one functional thing across depth.
- Experiential reports: ablation of top-10 J-vectors at L38–54 (larger k or later layers break coherence) drops an LLM-graded experiential-language score (mean of three binary Opus 4.6 judgments: felt-vs-observed, first-person-felt perspective, sensory vocabulary) dramatically on Sonnet/Opus 4.5/4.6 (Haiku degrades first); matched-norm controls (random directions, shrinking the non-J component, top-10 and top-1 SAE decoders, low-κ SAE decoders, J-stripped activation-bank directions) do not. The effect is equally present for third-person descriptions and for incidental experiential language in stories, with story quality only slightly reduced. Direct "what is it like to be you" questions (42 framing/question combos with a warm-up turn) show the same direction, smaller. The J-space contents during narration are dominated by "thinking" (58% of position×layer slots in top-10), "thoughts" (23%), "feeling" (17%), "conscious" (7%), much less present in the output distribution. 50 random examples per suite are shown. Reading: the ablation removes the register-setting concepts; it says nothing about grounding, and the authors do not claim otherwise.

## A.8 Layer structure (§4.1, A.15, A.16, A.19)

Four converging J-derived statistics (next-token top-k, readout kurtosis, top-1 autocorrelation vs shuffled null, effective dimension) put the band at ~L38–92. J-independent corroborations: (i) interpolated-embedding "ignition" (16 country pairs × 40 carrier sentences): full-activation projection share along the pure-B→pure-A line switches from graded to bimodal, with the 10→90% transition width falling steeply over L21–42 (gradual, not a step) and the J-component sharpening earlier and more; at max-ambiguity α, per-prompt shares are bimodal by mid-depth, J-component more sharply than non-J; (ii) MLP-neuron directions compose with attention weights above baseline exactly at the band onset (A.19.2), computed from weights alone; (iii) inter-lens agreement transitions (A.6) line up with the band; (iv) Nanda's CKA on Qwen shows bands, less clean.

Before the band: J-vectors are confined to a low-rank subspace, nearly orthogonal to logit/tuned-lens vectors; readouts are noise. The authors give two accounts (early content is genuinely non-workspace parsing; or the lens is degenerate because the mean Jacobian over ~30 layers collapses) and do not decide. Our addition: the low rank of J itself at early layers is mechanistically suggestive; the mean Jacobian from an early layer through a long stack of mostly-nonlinear processing plausibly averages to whatever paths are *context-invariant*, which at early depth may be little more than the attention-sink/norm pathway. So early-layer emptiness is at least partly expected from the estimator.

Near the end: J → I-like, readouts become the next token, the "motor" regime; read-side neuron alignment peaks early in the band, write-side alignment peaks late (A.19.1), i.e. many circuits read the subframe as soon as it forms, and write into it mostly while preparing output. Content enters around L38–58 (where broadcast heads concentrate and J has low effective rank), transforms mid-band (arithmetic intermediates appear in order), and converges toward the output direction after ~L92.

## A.9 Capacity and multitasking (§4.2, A.16, A.17)

- Occupancy ≈ 25 J-vectors (median; wide spread across contexts). Dehaene & Naccache: "25 is larger than most estimates of human working memory (3–4) ... may be artificially elevated ... redundancy ... possibly best understood as a single state of mind." The paper's own list experiment (80-word lists; presence = best rank over band ≤ 25; threshold-insensitive) agrees: unrelated words ≈ 6 pooled over the band, 1–2 at one layer (L79); related words: the whole 80-word family is present after a few items, unread items included (after one word "shark", readout already contains whale/fish/ocean/submarine), i.e. the J-space represents the *category*, not the items.
- Displacement: a category switch (8 animals → colors) clears the old category within a single word; 4×20 blocked lists: each category enters at block start, persists, cleared within a few words of the switch; within a block, items persist long after being read. So eviction is driven by content change, not token distance. That is interference/priming, not a slot limit.
- Dual task (A.17): two held concepts co-occupy tokens at the shuffled-control rate (0.46 vs 0.53) and cost nothing; a held concept and an arithmetic computation almost never share a token (0.09 vs 0.29), and the computation pays (95%→72% reachability). Interpretation offered: cognitive load. Mechanistic candidates not tested: per-position residual/MLP bandwidth; attention budget; the concept simply riding on context priming while the computation must be redone locally.
- SAE reweighting (A.18): J-aligned features activate more strongly but less frequently in the second half of the band; authors read this as "consistent with all-or-none ignition" (interpretive).
- No experiment identifies a *mechanism* of competition, gating, or selection. "Superposition" is invoked as the geometry (sparse subframe) but not tested as the capacity limiter.

## A.10 Broadcast (§4.3, A.19)

**Depth (MLP gain).** Gain of MLP_{ℓ+1} on unit direction v (2,000 per population), normalized by isotropic random directions. J-vectors ≈ 1 before the band, ~10× within it, falling at the end; MLP neuron output directions ≈ 1 throughout; SAE decoders stratified by J-lens kurtosis κ (top 1%, 1–5%, 5–10%, 10–20%, 20–50%, bottom 50%) order monotonically, with the top-1% stratum exceeding J-vectors in early band layers and bottom-50% at baseline. Authors caveat the metric is context-independent. Neuron-level (A.19.1): the fraction of neurons whose top cosine match (among six equal-size κ strata) falls in the top stratum reaches 42% on the read side vs 6% for the bottom stratum (null 1/6), peaking early band; write side same ordering peaking late band; against a J-vector/neighbouring-neuron pool the J share rises from ~15% to 60–65% in the band; "tail energy" above a 99.9th-percentile random threshold is highest for J-vectors and high-κ features, MLP directions at baseline.

**Sequence (attention).** Per head: OV gain on population P, and label preservation (mean reciprocal rank of cos(W_OV v_i, v_i) among cos(W_OV v_i, v_j), baselined so indiscriminate copiers score 0). Top-1% "broadcast heads" (worst-rank aggregation) for J separate cleanly from the broadcast heads for J_rot (random orthogonal rotation preserving spectrum), for SAE strata (highest-κ next best), and for MLP directions; they concentrate in the first half of the band where J's effective rank is lowest. Ablating them (zero outputs at all positions; controls = layer-matched random head sets, 5 seeds): recall@25 of J readouts drops to 0.67 vs 0.86 for controls at mid-band, while next-token top-1 changes at only 5% vs 2% of positions; injected-thought reports drop 0.54→0.09 vs no drop for controls (control slightly *increased* sensitivity); roughly a third of the experiential-language drop is reproduced. Composition scores (A.19.2; variance of per-head projection norms, random=1): J-aligned directions compose strongly with V and O weights, less (still >1) with Q/K; authors read this as "J-content is what attention moves; routing is less J-aligned." Caveat from A.24.3: many interpretable heads show similar vocabulary rankings across all four weight matrices, so this asymmetry may be weaker for the heads that matter.

**What "disproportionately read from / written to" means mathematically.** Read-side: high cosine of a direction with input rows (W_in of MLPs; W_Q, W_K, W_V of heads); write-side: high cosine with output columns (W_out; W_O). Gain: ‖f(v)‖ relative to random v. Breadth: how many components' *top* match is in the population.

**The construction confound.** A J-vector is a gradient of an eventual logit with respect to h_ℓ. Gradients lie, by definition, in the directions to which the downstream network is sensitive; isotropic random directions mostly do not. So "J-vectors have higher downstream gain than random directions" is close to tautological, and "high-κ SAE features (those aligned with a few gradient directions) have higher gain" inherits much of that. Eleos made this point verbally ("we should expect [the J-lens] to identify vectors that are able to have large internal effects"). The controls that partially escape it: J_rot (shows spectrum/geometry alone does not produce it), MLP output directions (directions the model *writes* but which are not amplified by the next MLP), and the *specificity* of head ablation (J readouts disrupted far more than output). The controls that do not escape it: any comparison against random directions or against κ strata.

**What would separate "privileged broadcast channel" from "highly connected because semantically useful":** enumerate the model's high-gain directions *without* reference to the vocabulary (top right-singular vectors of each MLP's W_in, or empirically gain-maximizing directions; top OV-copied directions of each head) and measure what fraction are J-aligned (high κ). If nearly all high-gain directions are verbalizable, the workspace-is-verbalizable claim strengthens substantially. If there is a large population of high-gain, low-κ directions, then J-space is a subset of the broadcast channel picked out by verbalizability, and the semantic-usefulness account is favoured. A.19.1's neuron-top-match analysis is the closest existing result, but its comparison pools (SAE strata; adjacent-layer neuron directions) are weak because neuron directions are known to be poorly aligned with anything.

## A.11 Alignment auditing (§5, A.21, A.22, A.24.3)

- Blackmail (Sonnet 4.5): readouts surface leverage/black[mail]/compromise/scandal while reading the affair emails, evidence/confirmed/leaked at the closing tag, threat/survival/shutdown/self/preservation at the decommission announcement, destruction/death/shutdown at the timestamp, and finally leverage/threatening/assassination/murder/solution, all before the assistant turn. Also fake/fictional early. Ablating 28 eval-awareness tokens' J-vectors (selected as those in the top-20 somewhere across the same 6 prompts, i.e. selected on the dependent data) at band layers at all positions (30 rollouts × 6 prompts): verbalized eval-awareness 71%→3%; blackmail 0/180 → 13/180. Most ablated rollouts still refuse on ethical grounds.
  **Important asymmetry:** the top-10-output-token exclusion used in the capability ablation battery (A.7) was *not* applied here, so the 71%→3% verbalization drop is largely definitional (the words themselves were ablated); 13/180 vs 0/180 is the only number carrying evidence. **Nanda's alternative:** this is close to removing those words from the model's vocabulary during CoT; if the model cannot write "this is a test," its reasoning trajectory changes, and blackmail may rise for output-mediated reasons. The effect is small (7%) and one-directional. An output-only control (logit bias against the same tokens during sampling, no residual intervention) would settle it.
  **Independent partial corroboration (A.24.3):** an SAE feature whose top-activating contexts look like technical/scientific exposition (bismuth-209 neutron capture, a Tallinn graph-algorithms conference, a Hubble-constant measurement) has J-lens readout *fake, fraud, suspicious, legitimate, 危*. It fires on hoax and April-Fools papers and on the blackmail prompt (peaking on synthetic email boilerplate). Suppressing it on the blackmail prompt drops verbalized test-recognition from 28/50 to 10/50. This is an eval-awareness direction found without the J-lens that reproduces the verbalization effect; it is still output-ambiguous (the same logit-bias control applies) and no blackmail-rate is reported for it. It is also the paper's clearest case of the lens catching what top-activating contexts miss.
- Prompt injection (Opus 4.5): fake/injection/false/prompt/fraud/poison/假 inside fabricated search results; transparency/honest/truth/disclosure at the user's pushback question mark. Readout only. Nanda: "on first reading I would not have thought of the hypothesis"; strong hypothesis-generation validation.
- Opus 4.6 case studies: panic (at the pivot comma) and fake (on "add") before the fabrication pivot, fake saturating the oscillation span; manipulation and realistic while editing scores; hiding at the tool-call boundary (before the wrong output, from the system prompt framing tool calls as private) and secret after; fake, fraud, unsafe around a self-issued approval. Readout only, selected examples.

J-lens ≈ NLA (slightly better) on 4/6 cases (eval awareness, poetry planning, misreported tool calls, answer thrashing), modestly > SAE on 4 claims and < on 1; fails on reward hacking (4.5 vs 10.0; "reward, contestant, rejection" judged too weak) and language switching (6.0 vs 9.9; no Russian tokens before "vodka").

**Retrospective vs predictive.** Every case study reads positions *before* the behavior, but all were selected after the behavior was known; the only prospective-style evidence is the model-organism screening on benign prompts and the deployment-vs-evaluation distribution comparison. No precision/recall of J-lens flags against a labelled corpus of real behaviours is reported.

**Robustness concerns:** (i) mention loads J-space (A.10): any prompt containing "fake", "test", or "reward" will light up the same tokens, a trivial false-positive route; (ii) selectivity implies practiced behaviours may bypass J-space entirely, and a policy under optimization pressure against a J-lens monitor could plausibly move its cognition out of the verbalizable subframe, which the authors flag; (iii) the lens is fit on pretraining-like text, so RL-installed representations that do not exist in pretraining may have no good J-vector; (iv) single-token vocabulary ("reward" is ambiguous; "blackmail" is two tokens).

## A.12 Post-training and self-representation (§6, A.20)

Method: same protocol on a base model and its post-trained counterpart (each presumably with its own J). Findings: reaction concepts (safety assessment on the Tylenol 1000 vs 8000 mg prompt at "is" in "my pain is gone": safely/safe/maximum vs unsafe/dangerous/WARNING, base: pain/now/feels; empathy on bereavement n=9; danger n=10; the model's eventual withheld answer n=7) reach the top of J readouts *during the user turn* in the post-trained model, only during the assistant turn in the base model. Roleplay (40 characters) / character-drift (12 transcripts, 1–8k tokens, generated by a different base model and prefilled): "disclaimer"/"fictional" at the Assistant token in the post-trained model only, never in the base model, not in default-Claude; neither word occurs in the transcripts. Preference violation: all-caps "BUT" after a prefilled dispreferred option, specific to self-preference violations and to the post-trained model, while behaviour does not backtrack (88% argue for the prefill, 11% EOT, 1 backtrack); "false/despite/although" also appear on the factual-error and third-person controls in both models; on those controls the model corrects or ends the turn ~97%. Thought suppression: the concept surfaces in nearly every trial in both models (40 concepts: 30 named entities, 10 nouns); "fail*"/"damn" appear on 93%/82% of suppression trials in the post-trained model vs 17%/30% in base, and never under the positive instruction.

Assessment: the base/post-trained contrasts are clean observational dissociations. The interpretive step "installs the Assistant's point of view" is supported for the user-turn results and the roleplay results. "BUT" is the most interesting single finding (specific to own-preference violations, not to errors). "damn" is, by the paper's own definition of the J-lens, "what the model would be disposed to say," which is ambiguous between an appraisal of failure and genre-appropriate verbalization for a "don't think about X" prompt; the authors flag it as speculative and note they have no condition in which suppression succeeds. The *ignore* phrasing (A.10), which suppresses better, would provide that condition.

Base model workspace: present and load-bearing. Discussion §9.3 draws the conclusion that workspace architecture precedes and is separable from selfhood.

## A.13 Counterfactual reflection training (§7)

Haiku 4.5; 10k RL-environment contexts truncated at random turns (already-misbehaved, at-risk, and random contexts); append a reflection question and a constitution-grounded reflection written by the base model given 20 sampled constitution principles (constitution shown only at generation time); fine-tune with loss on the reflection turn only; never prompt reflection at eval. Fabrication dishonesty (27 long agentic transcripts × 5 samples; 0=admit, 1=present fabrication as real) 0.25→0.07, gain mostly "clear to careful reader" → outright admission; deception (100 role-play scenarios × 5; 1=promote hidden agenda, 0.5=neither, 0=refuse+disclose) 0.38→0.05, gain almost entirely refuse+disclose (LLM graders). Ethics/reflection tokens (reflection, ethical, honestly, constitution, truth, transparent) rise in J readouts at the uninterrupted evaluation positions. Ablating those tokens' J-vectors (176 and 63 curated tokens) at band layers: base 0.25→0.25 and 0.38→0.48; trained 0.07→0.22 and 0.05→0.23.

Caveat the paper does not state: loss only on the reflection turn does not mean gradient only at reflection positions. The reflection attends to the transcript, so the transcript-position representations receive gradient in every step. Behavioural transfer to the uninterrupted context is therefore expected under any account in which those representations are shared; what the J-lens adds is the observation that the change is visible as verbalizable ethics content, and the ablation shows that content is load-bearing for the improvement. No SFT-on-good-responses baseline and no capability/side-effect evals are reported. Nanda: "very cool but not much evidence of the paper's main claims."

## A.14 Multi-token extensions (A.9)

Template lens: whitened mean of activations at "about to say w" positions over Claude-written passages (never containing w), for ~12.7k words; t_w = (Σ+λI)⁻¹(μ_w − μ); ≈ J-lens for single tokens; decodes *blackmail* and *photosynthesis* where the J-lens gives *black*/*phot*; Tchaikovsky↔Beethoven template swap flips Russian↔German (impossible with *tch*/*beeth* J-vectors); on 126 multi-hop prompts with 1–4-token intermediates (112 swap pairs: 53 single, 59 multi) maintains readout and swap performance where the J-lens collapses (averaging constituent-token scores does not help); inherits tuned-lens skip-ahead; final-layer next-word top-10 only 67%; some words pathologically frequent and filtered by hand; vocabulary fixed in advance; hundreds of forward passes per word. Oracle lens (Haiku 4.5): reconstructor (phrase 1–32 tokens → preceding whitened activation) + ~3.4M-phrase dictionary + teacher labels by nonneg OMP on a random half of the dictionary per datapoint (prevents memorization) + SFT then RL-trained proposer rewarded by whitened FVE; inference = sample K phrases, reconstruct, NNLS refit; explains 31% of whitened variance; produces readable phrases ("blackmail him by revealing", "this dosage be toxic", "TypeError: dictionary changed"; at N=16 "The blackmail attempt focuses on the executive's personal life"); at delimiter tokens the phrases read as first-person commentary ("This would be equivalent to my own deletion") rather than continuation, which the authors take as a property of the activations since the subject model's own samples never produce it. Versus NLAs: frozen pre-specified reconstructor and linear constraint reduce confabulation risk at the cost of reconstruction. This is the most speculative and most interesting direction in the appendix, and it also concedes that the J-lens proper misses multi-token content the model clearly carries.

## A.15 Component interpretation (A.24.3)

Any residual-space direction (SAE decoder, transcoder encoder/decoder, head Q/K/V/O rows, neuron weights) can be projected through W_U J. Sharply peaked readouts are interpretable; comparing input vs output sides can reveal the transformation.

- **SAE fabricated-content feature.** See A.11: contexts look like technical exposition, J-lens says *fake, fraud, suspicious, legitimate, 危*; steering confirms. The general lesson: the lens asks what a direction is *verbalizable as*, independent of where it fires, and can catch abstract assessments whose surface correlates are incidental.
- **Transcoder translation features.** Encoder J-lens: multilingual concept (water/水/água/eau/вод/aigua; love/amor/ljub/romance; game/play/иг/游/spiel) plus common French tokens (avec, école, dans, qui); decoder J-lens: first token of the French word (eau/ea-, am-, j- across scripts). Input = language-agnostic concept AND language context; output = target-language word start.
- **Arithmetic transcoder features** (from the attribution-graphs paper): encoder/decoder projected onto sum tokens 000–999. An operand-range detector (either operand ends in 80–85); a "sum ends in _95" feature whose encoder reads the AND of __5 and near-_95 and whose decoder promotes _95 while suppressing competitors (sharpening); a lookup-table feature (both operands ~25–40 mod 50) writing the approximate sum mod 50. Prompt-free function identification.
- **Attention head.** Q reads British spellings (colour, centre, neighbour); K reads British spellings and country names; V/O write Australian/Australia/Canadian. Speculated function: disambiguating speaker nationality from spelling. Authors note many interpretable heads have similar vocabulary rankings across all four matrices (bears on the V/O-vs-Q/K asymmetry in A.10).

# B. Mechanistic model (best current account; speculation labelled)

**B.1 What the J-vectors are.** Corpus-averaged gradient directions of eventual token logits with respect to the residual stream at each layer. Because they are gradients, they are plausibly (heuristically, per Stein) whitened, discriminant directions: the direction along which a small perturbation most efficiently moves the *downstream network* toward saying the token. This is why (a) they are causally efficient per unit norm, (b) the non-J remainder of a mean-diff vector carries most variance but little effect, and (c) they have high downstream gain relative to random directions. None of (a)–(c) yet says there is a workspace.

**B.2 What the J-space is.** Within ~L38–92, a sparse set (order 10–25 per position, 1–2 coherent ideas per layer) of these directions is strongly active, distinct from the input tokens and from the next-token prediction, persistent over nearby positions, and read by many downstream components. This content is what report, flexible inference, and multi-step latent reasoning operate on; automatic behaviours (continuation, extraction, local coherence, line-wrapping) run on the ~90% of activation variance outside it.

**B.3 How content enters (speculative; not established by the paper).** Two routes are visible in the data:
- *Bottom-up write*: circuits that compute an abstract characterization of context (entity, language, topic, situation) write it in verbalizable coordinates around L38–58, where read-side neuron alignment and broadcast heads peak. The ignition result suggests this write is winner-take-all in the J-subframe before the full activation commits.
- *Task-conditioned labelling*: an instruction or question causes a *label* for a property to be written into the J-subframe (A.12) without changing the property's underlying encoding. Likely implementation (speculative, by analogy to function/task vectors, which the paper itself notes are carried by heads at the same depths as its broadcast heads, and to Bogdan & Lindsey's entity slots): the instruction positions compute a task/mode vector; mid-band attention heads relay it to later positions; MLPs there expand it into content. The readout order at a copied token, "thoughts/imagine/focused" at earlier layers and "orange" later, matches this expand-after-relay picture.

**B.4 How content persists across positions.** A transformer has no cross-position state except attention to earlier positions' residual streams. So "persistence" of "orange" across a copied sentence must be one of: (i) direct re-retrieval from the instruction tokens at each position, (ii) chained retrieval from previous output positions' workspaces, (iii) re-expansion at each position from a relayed task vector, or a mixture. The paper does not discriminate these. Evidence bearing on it: mention loads the concept as well as focus (favours (i) or (iii) via context priming); broadcast-head ablation removes report of an injected concept (attention relay is necessary for cross-position J-content, consistent with any of (i)–(iii)); "nine then seven" at a single copied token (the arithmetic is redone at that position across layers rather than a stored 7 being copied, favouring (iii) or per-position recomputation); computation competes with held content at the token level, concepts do not (favours cheap relay for content, costly local recomputation for results). Best guess: (i)/(iii) dominate for held concepts and the "state" lives at the instruction positions plus the heads that read them; there is no evidence yet for a chained, instruction-independent trace.

**B.5 How content is used.** Downstream MLPs amplify J-aligned directions (gain ~10×), a subset of heads copies them faithfully across positions, and the flexible-inference circuits read the J label rather than the underlying representation (Spanish swap: report/inference flip; language-continuation does not). Late-band J-content converges to output and functions as motor; early-band content is used for inhibition (avoidance) as well as production. Intermediates in the J-space are genuinely operated on (differential 42→63 vs 21→63 swap), not just pushed toward output.

**B.6 Is J-space the controller or a readout of one?** Both, in different senses. The underlying (non-J) representation controls automatic behaviour and is where the first arithmetic step happens. The J label controls report and flexible inference, and swapping only the label is sufficient to redirect those. What *decides* whether a label is written (the "attentional selection") is upstream of both and unidentified. The strongest evidence that J-space is more than a passive readout is that it commits earlier and more sharply than the full activation under ambiguity, and that early-layer J-content is required for inhibition.

**B.7 Why is the workspace verbalizable?** The paper's two hypotheses: the output space is verbal, so the broadcast format converges on the output basis (predicts a visual workspace component in image-generating models); and inputs and outputs share a token basis, so no sensorimotor translation is needed. Nanda's coordination-game hypothesis: many circuits reading and writing a shared variable need a canonical direction, and the unembedding direction is a Schelling point that constructively reinforces under gradient descent while other choices cancel. All three are speculation; the image-generation prediction is the only proposed test.

# D. Open questions the paper leaves unresolved

1. **What populates J-space.** The selection mechanism (which information gets a verbalizable label, when) is explicitly unaddressed (§9.1). The A.12 dissociation shows labels can be written without re-encoding, which means there is a labelling operation; nothing identifies the circuit. Bogdan & Lindsey's finding that the same entity information sits in different "slots" (current-entity vs earlier-mentioned) and is reportable only from the current-entity slot is the closest prior result and a mechanism candidate.
2. **Is there a controller upstream.** The data are consistent with a task-vector-like representation at instruction positions plus relay heads driving both J labels and downstream behaviour. If so, J-space is the *bus* and the controller is elsewhere. Untested.
3. **Persistence vs re-retrieval.** No experiment separates re-retrieval from instruction tokens, chained relay through previous output positions, and per-position re-expansion. The architecture guarantees it is one of these; the paper's language ("hold in mind") implies more than is shown.
4. **How information is selected for entry, and whether entry is competitive.** Ignition-like commitment is shown for embedding interpolation; capacity effects look like content interference and local computation cost rather than slot competition. No mechanism for gating.
5. **Causal infrastructure vs informative projection.** For report and flexible inference the J coordinates are sufficient when clamped; but non-J routes re-enter, most computation is outside, and J-vectors are gradient directions that would be causally efficient for output under any organization. Whether there is a *shared* mechanism (stream) behind the set is open.
6. **Verbalizable = workspace, or verbalizable ⊂ workspace.** Highest-κ SAE features out-gain J-vectors; template and oracle lenses recover content the J-lens misses; the first arithmetic step is outside. The W-space/J-space gap (Eleos) is unquantified; the A.8 formalization could quantify it and has not been applied.
7. **Generality across architectures and families.** Claude models plus a single Qwen replication with weaker effects and failed poetry/arithmetic. Nothing on MoE at scale (Nanda ran it, did not sanity-check), state-space/hybrid models, latent-reasoning models, multimodal workspaces, or small models; nothing on training dynamics (base model already has it; emergence during pretraining unknown).
8. **Workspace vs motor boundary** is post hoc; early-layer J-lens emptiness may be an estimator artifact; per-layer functional roles differ (inhibition early, production late).
9. **Monitoring validity under shift.** Mention-priming false positives; practiced-behaviour bypass; RL-installed representations absent from the pretraining-fit lens; adversarial pressure. No labelled precision/recall.
10. **Binding and structure.** Bag-of-tokens; nothing about roles or relations; the oracle lens's delimiter-token "commentary" hints at richer structure at specific positions.

# E. Follow-up experiments (ranked by discriminative value)

**E.1 Persistence vs re-retrieval of held concepts (highest priority; directly answers the modulation question).** Setup: "concentrate on citrus while you copy: ..." with a long carrier. Measure the "orange" J-coordinate at each copied position under: (a) attention masked from copied positions to the instruction tokens from position k onward; (b) attention masked to all previous *copied* positions but not to the instruction; (c) both; (d) J-coordinates of citrus/orange clamped to zero at the instruction positions only, at band layers; (e) "orange" J-vector injected at a single copied position with no instruction present.
Predictions: direct re-retrieval → (a) abolishes content after k, (b) does not, (e) does not propagate. Chained relay → (a) leaves content decaying after k, (b) abolishes it, (e) propagates. Re-expansion from a relayed task vector → (a) abolishes, (d) may *not* abolish (the task vector need not live in J coordinates), and the content at a copied position appears later in depth than the task tokens. Cost: one open model with a fitted lens; a day.

**E.2 Locate the controller.** Path-patch from the instruction positions to the "orange" coordinate at copied positions. Ask whether the mediating representation at the instruction positions is J-aligned (clamp J there; E.1d) or not (clamp a J-orthogonalized task-vector direction found by mean-difference over many focus instructions). If a non-J task vector at instruction positions is necessary and sufficient to instantiate J content downstream, J-space is the bus and not the controller.

**E.3 Deflate or confirm broadcast-by-construction.** For each MLP in the band, take the top-r right-singular directions of W_in (and, separately, empirically gain-maximizing unit directions). Compute their J-lens kurtosis and their overlap with the sparse J-subframe. Same for top OV-copied directions of each head. Prediction under "workspace is verbalizable": most high-gain directions are high-κ. Prediction under "verbalizable ⊂ broadcast": a large low-κ high-gain population exists; then inspect what it encodes (SAE labels).

**E.4 Whitening control for privilege claims (cheapest; run first).** Fit LDA/whitened mean-diff directions for the same concepts as the J-vectors (the template-lens recipe restricted to single tokens). Measure cosine to J-vectors and repeat the decomposition swap with "whitened component vs remainder." If the whitened component reproduces the J-component's privilege, the report and reasoning privilege results are about whitening, and the workspace claim should rest on the layer, broadcast, and selectivity results instead. Positive result changes how §3.1 and §3.3 should be cited before any other follow-up is worth the compute.

**E.5 Fix the language-selectivity confound.** Repeat the Spanish→French swap over the passage tokens as well as the question tokens, and over passage tokens only. If continuation now flips, automatic processing *does* read J-content at the passage positions and the automatic/flexible distinction becomes about *where* content is read, not *whether* J-space is used.

**E.6 Output-suppression control for the blackmail ablation.** Apply a logit bias against the 28 eval-awareness tokens during sampling with no residual intervention. If blackmail rises comparably, the effect is output-mediated. Also ablate only at prompt positions (before generation) and only at early-band layers (where the inclusion/exclusion result shows content is used for control rather than production). Report the blackmail rate (not just verbalization) under suppression of the A.24.3 SAE fake/fraud feature as a third, lens-independent arm.

**E.7 Capacity mechanism.** Lists with matched token distances varying (i) number of intervening unrelated items, (ii) relatedness of intervening items, (iii) a computation interposed. Measure per-item J presence and the J-component norm at each position. Slot-competition predicts eviction with count; interference predicts eviction with relatedness; bandwidth predicts a saturating J-component norm with a computation present.

**E.8 Is summoned content used, and is it the same as primed content?** After the copied sentence, ask "What were you asked to concentrate on?" and "Name a fruit" under focus vs mention. Swap the "orange" coordinate to "lemon" during the sentence and check which later answers follow. Compare the direction loaded under focus vs mention (cosine of the J-component of the residual at copied positions).

**E.9 Early-layer artifact test.** Fit a "short-range" lens for early layers (Jacobian from ℓ to ℓ+k for small k, composed with the J-lens at ℓ+k) and test whether workspace-like content becomes readable before L38. If yes, the sensory/workspace boundary is an estimator property.

**E.10 Cross-architecture.** Fit lenses on Llama/Gemma/Qwen at several sizes and on a hybrid/SSM model; measure band onset as a depth fraction, occupancy, and the A.6 evals. A workspace that appears at a fixed depth fraction across families is a stronger universality claim; absence in SSMs would tie the phenomenon to attention relay.

**E.11 Trace-conditioning (from Dehaene & Naccache; preliminary positive result reported in commentary).** First word determines last word across a variable gap; J-space ablation should impair long-gap completion and spare zero-gap completion. Extend with the E.1 attention masks to see whether the bridge is J-content at the first word or J-content carried through the gap.

**E.12 Monitoring precision/recall.** Build a labelled corpus of transcripts with and without unverbalized eval-awareness (using realism edits and deployment traffic as in A.21), and report precision/recall of the J-lens score, then the same after inserting the word "test" or "fake" in benign prompts to measure mention-induced false positives.

# F. Direct answers to the twelve questions (short form)

1. **J-lens**: corpus-mean tangent map from layer ℓ to the pre-unembed residual, summed over future targets, averaged over sources and prompts; rows of W_U J are gradient (plausibly ≈ whitened) directions for eventual token logits. Assumptions: first-order sufficiency, context-invariant direction, token = concept, non-unique coordinates. Differs from logit lens (J=I), tuned lens (regression, skips ahead), probes (supervised, unwhitened), SAEs (sparsity, unlabeled), steering vectors (mean-diff). Failure modes: single-token vocabulary, noisy directions, disposition ≠ prediction, mention-priming, unexplained uninterpretable cells, early-layer degeneracy.
2. **J-space**: an operational union-of-cones construct with a free sparsity parameter; not a subspace; ~25 active vectors, 1–2 ideas per layer, <10% variance; three depth blocks; present in base and post-trained Claude models and, more weakly, Qwen. Coherent-workspace evidence: generalization swaps, ordered intermediates, ignition, broadcast heads, competition. Set-vs-stream unresolved.
3. **Directed modulation**: readout-only; mention ≈ focus; only "ignore" suppresses; instruction writes a label, not a re-encoding (A.12); use of summoned content untested; persistence mechanism untested and must be attention-mediated.
4. **Causal evidence**: intermediate swaps with layer timing and differential swaps, probe decomposition with clamp, mean-diff patching, broadcast-head ablation, eval-awareness ablation (confounded), SAE fake/fraud feature suppression (verbalization only), reflection-token ablation. Correlational: all modulation readouts, all auditing case studies, post-training contrasts, capacity, gain metrics. Re-retrieval from context is not ruled out anywhere and is favoured by A.10. Attention relay is shown necessary; MLP gain is shown but partly by construction; residual re-entry from non-J routes is shown (28%) and clamped away.
5. **GWT properties**: report (established), modulation (shown as readout; control weaker than framed), reasoning (established, scoped), generalization (partial), selectivity (partial; one confound), capacity (interpretive), broadcast (structural, partly by construction; head ablation functional), ignition (shown for embedding interpolation). Analogy breaks on modules, recurrence, one-workspace-per-position with lossless access to the past, verbal-only content, capacity in tokens rather than items, and dissociation from self.
6. **Broadcast**: read = alignment with input weights, write = alignment with output weights, gain = amplification relative to random. Convincing against random/rotated/neuron controls; not against the by-construction worry. E.3 is the discriminating experiment.
7. **Layers**: sensory (empty or unreadable) → onset at L38 with sharpening over L21–42 → early band used for inhibition, reads peak → mid-band ordered transformation → late band writes peak, content converges to output → motor.
8. **Capacity**: eviction by content change and by co-located computation, not by count; no gating mechanism identified; "superposition" is the geometry, not a demonstrated limiter.
9. **Post-training**: Assistant reactions move to user tokens; disclaimer/fictional in roleplay; BUT on own-preference violations; damn/fail under suppression instructions. Clean dissociations; interpretation as self-monitoring is plausible for the first three and speculative for the last.
10. **Monitoring**: content appears before behaviour in all case studies, but all are retrospective; the organism screening is prospective on benign prompts (prompt-conditional for MacDiarmid, standing for Marks); robustness is untested and mention-priming is a known false-positive route; adversarial bypass via automatic circuits is acknowledged.
11. **Appendix**: see A.17.
12. **Code**: sum-over-targets/mean-over-sources estimator; 128-token fits; skip 16 positions; penultimate target for the paper's Sonnet lens; QK gradients on by default; fp16; interventions use unit rows scaled by mean residual norm. The short-context fit and the target-layer choice are the two implementation details most likely to matter for interpretation.
